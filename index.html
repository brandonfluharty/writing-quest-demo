The model · Human first, Professional second, AI third

Before any of the steps below, anchor on this. It's the order I run everything in, and it's the reason my system works.

1 · Human first

You lived it. You say it out loud, in your own words, before any machine touches it. Voice notes, a whiteboard photo, the story you told me on our call. That's the source.

2 · Professional second

You know who it's for and what it has to do. The reader. Your point of view. Your standard. Twenty years of selling taught you this. Write it down once so the machine can work inside it.

3 · AI third

It's last on purpose. If you start with AI, you skip the part where you become worth listening to. The machine doesn't write and then you edit. You set the standard, and it works inside it.

Knowing more was the old game. Orchestrating better is the new one. You stop asking for outputs and start designing a system: what goes in, who does what, what gets checked, and where you step in. That's the difference between being a worker in the chat and being the orchestrator of it. With a good system I feel like I've got ten people working for me.

The words I'll use on this page

Project — one place in Claude for one body of work. The instructions and files inside it load every time. You stop re-explaining yourself.
Skill — a plain-English file of rules. "Never say this. Always show the old line and the new line." When you like an output and know you'll want it again, you save it as a skill. You've already done this with your account plan template.
Node — one job. Think of it as an agent with one specialty. One node absorbs your sources. One proposes lines. One cuts. You want a specialist as a node, not one agent doing a bunch of writing and editing.
Agent — a node that runs on its own once you kick it off. Same thing, different word. Don't let the word scare you. It's a plain-English file with one job.
Graph — the order the nodes hand off in. Voice notes go to Absorb. Absorb hands to Propose. Propose hands to Cut. Cut hands to Pangram. Then it comes back to you. You start it. You check it. You decide what survives.
Propose only — the rule that makes it Pro. The machine never writes a line of your prose from scratch. It proposes a line, shows where it came from, and waits.
VOICE file — a skill that holds how you sound and the words you never use. Every node reads it first. When you catch Claude doing something you'd never do, you add one line. You never say it again.

ABSORB

The one move · Baseline → Pro

This is one move, broken into pieces you can do from your phone. Do them in order. When the last one is checked, you're Pro.

Make one Project for your book

One place. The instructions load every time. You stop telling Claude who you are in every chapter chat.

Project instructions template
You are helping me, [Your Name], write my book, [Your Book Title].

WHO I AM: [Your role, in one line]. [The one moment your story turns on, in one line]. [What you rebuilt to, with a number]. [Family, in three words]. [What you do on the side].

WHO MY READER IS: [The one person you're writing for, in one sentence].

MY POINT OF VIEW: [Where you break from the conventional advice in your field, in two sentences].

THE ORDER OF WORK: I speak first (voice dump). You never write prose from scratch. You propose lines built from my own words and show where each one came from. I decide what survives.

NEVER: invent a fact or a feeling I didn't state. Praise a passage without saying exactly why. Use these words: leverage (as a verb), journey, unlock, game-changer, stress-test, "let that sink in."

ALWAYS: contractions. Short sentences. End most paragraphs on an ordinary sentence, not a punchline. One metaphor per section at most.

Propose/dispose rule
From now on you never draft my prose from scratch. For every section:
1. Quote the exact lines from my voice dump you are working from.
2. Propose a revised line. Show: OLD → NEW → WHY (one sentence).
3. Mark anything only I can write as [I WRITE] with one line on what it needs to do.
4. Stop and wait for my decision before moving on.
